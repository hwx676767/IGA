Entry I
March 14, 2042

Today, we implanted the first complete Synapse prototype into a volunteer. It was such a success, exceeding all my expectations.

It could remember what a person saw, what they heard, and even calculate the best possible choice when they hesitated. This was what we set out to build. Not a command. Not a means of control. Just a way to make sure no one would miss an opportunity simply because they were afraid, poor, or uninformed.

---
Entry II

They were no longer asking what Synapse could do.

They were asking: What can Synapse make people do?

They wanted behavioral prediction.
When someone would quit their job.
When someone might divorced.
When someone might commit a crime.

---
Entry III
November 19, 2045

We signed the founding charter today. Twelve of us around a table that cost more than my father's house. They call it the Falcon Club — someone joked that a falcon can see a mouse from a mile up and decide, in half a second, whether it lives or dies. Nobody laughed the second time he said it.

I didn't care about the name. I cared about the work. A system that remembers for you, protects you, never lets you forget the people you love — I have spent my whole life building toward this morning. My son just turned twelve years old yesterday. I want to build him a world where no one disappears without a trace.


---
Entry IV
June, 2046


The Falcon Club finally made me understand what they were really after.

Government officials, investors, medical executives, and several people I had never seen listed in any public record were sitting around the same table. They said Synapse had to become essential infrastructure.

I asked what would happen if someone refused to use it.

Someone at the other end of the table laughed. Elena said, “Then make life increasingly difficult for people who don’t.”

Later, someone reassured me that nobody would be forced to use Synapse. However, people would simply choose the only option that made sense.

I haven't been able to forget that sentence.


---
Entry V

They wanted something they called “compliance optimization.”

The name was almost elegant.


---
Entry VI
November 20, 2048

..the club held its anniversary dinner last night. Champagne, a string quartet, a toast to "a safer, more predictable population." One of the founders — I won't write his name here, not yet — leaned over and told me, almost kindly, that the public doesn't want freedom. They want to never have to decide anything difficult again. He said Synapse doesn't take that decision away from people. It just makes the decision for them, quietly, before they notice there was one to make.

I said nothing. I smiled and drank the champagne like a coward.

...(three lines water-damaged, unreadable)...

I keep thinking about my son's face the day he was born, how certain I was that I was building him a shield. I am starting to understand that a shield and a cage look identical from the inside.


---
Entry VII
January 3, 2050

They're cleaning the original developers out. First, the accesses were reduced. Then the laboratory. Then the meetings. My name has disappeared from the project documents three times now. They say it's part of a “restructuring of the core team.”
I know what that means.
Several people who worked with me from the beginning have already left. Some moved away. Some resigned. Some...I don't even know where they are now.

---
Entry VIII
April 20, 2050

The accident finally happened.

I should have been angry. Instead, I was afraid.

Because I knew this wasn't malfunction.


---
Entry IX
April 23, 2050

I am struggled. 

I copied the most important files that I secretly kept all along the developing.

If National Security ask why I waited this long, I don't know what I'll say.

Maybe I kept believing that as long as the system could still be repaired, none of this counted as betrayal. I believed that the people who create something have a responsibility to make it better. But it no longer belongs to the people who created it.


---
Entry X
April 26, 2050

I didn't do it. 
I'm afraid they've known all along.
This morning, my laboratory access was revoked. I also discovered that parts of my memory archive had been reorganized. I know I never deleted them. But something is gone.
---
Entry XI
May 8, 2050

If you are reading this, it means I never found a way to send it, or I ran out of time.

I am writing this by hand, in a book with no chip, no signal, nothing Synapse can hear. It is the only honest thing I own.

I want to say I fought harder. I didn't. I made the smaller, safer choice a hundred times, and I told myself each time it was temporary, until temporary became the whole shape of my life. That is the part of this story I most need someone to know — not the club, not the flagging layer, not the names. The cowardice. It was ordinary. It happened one quiet, reasonable decision at a time, and that is exactly how they built it to happen.

To whoever finds this — to my son, if it's him — I am sorry I gave you a world I already knew was wrong, and called it a gift.


---
Entry XII

Do not trust the first memory the system gives you. Or the second.

If you remember someone never existed in record, find them.
If someone suddenly stops being remembered by anyone, do not forget them with the rest.

And if you find a life inside your own memory that you know you never lived, don't be so quick to call it a malfunction. “malfunction” is simply the word the system uses when it admits that it was wrong.


---
Entry XIII

If someone finds this diary, don't believe it.
Verify it yourself.
Look through the old archives.
Find the people the system decided were unimportant. 
Ask the ones who were never meant to be heard. 
They know things the system doesn't want them to know.




BREAKING: Synapse Holds Emergency Press Conference After Memory-Storage Outage, Dodges Key Questions

By Lisa Park
April 20, 2050

Synapse Corporation held an emergency press conference this morning, acknowledging what executives called a "critical operational failure" following a massive cloud outage that left thousands of users temporarily unable to access portions of their stored memories. While the company issued a formal apology and pledged full restoration support for all affected users, executives repeatedly declined to answer reporters' questions about the root cause of the failure, the underlying system vulnerabilities, or the long-term implications for user privacy and data security.
When a reporter asked directly whether the outage exposed a fundamental flaw in Synapse's storage architecture, CEO Elena Vasquez offered only a carefully worded non-answer:
"We understand the concern, and we take full responsibility for what happened. But until our internal review is complete, we won't speculate on causes. What I can say is that our engineers are working around the clock, and restoring our users' trust is our top priority."
Pressed further on whether similar vulnerabilities could trigger future outages, Vasquez declined to elaborate, repeating only that "answers would come in time." Other executives on stage offered similarly guarded responses, prompting visible frustration among the assembled press corps.

Background: The Outage
The crisis began on April 18, when an unexplained disruption struck Synapse's cloud servers. Users across the network reported disorientation and sudden gaps in recollection, with many unable to retrieve memories they had previously entrusted to the company's storage system. By that night, the disruption had sharply worsened, tearing through Synapse's global memory network and triggering widespread panic. Users in multiple regions simultaneously reported acute memory loss and system-wide instability. Emergency services in several major cities were briefly overwhelmed by the surge in distress calls, as affected individuals described the experience as a complete severance from their recent personal history — some unable to recall even the events of the past few days.
In the hours immediately following the outage, Synapse remained largely silent. A company spokesperson declined to comment, and neither the CEO nor other senior executives would answer questions about the scope of the failure or whether lost memories could be recovered — a silence that only deepened public anxiety ahead of this morning's press conference.

What Comes Next
Regulators have announced plans for a formal investigation, examining Synapse's data security protocols, cloud architecture, and the safeguards meant to prevent memory loss or corruption. Depending on what investigators find, the company could face significant penalties.
The outage — and Synapse's evasive handling of it — has reignited a broader public debate about entrusting private corporations with something as intimate as human memory. Authorities say more information will be released as the investigation unfolds. As of this writing, Synapse's stock has taken a sharp hit, and calls for an independent audit of the company's memory infrastructure are mounting by the hour.
